# Balamonis Studio: Master Plan 2026–2027

**Prepared:** August 12, 2026  
**Scope:** Balamonis Studio, Balamonis Originals, creator web application, social revenue, film distribution, and the future OTT platform  
**Operating principle:** Build excellent work first, turn the repeatable system into software second, and launch owned distribution only after audience demand is proven.

---

## 1. Executive decision

Build one company with four layers, in this order:

1. **Balamonis Studio — cash flow and reputation.** Produce cinematic brand films, launch campaigns, trailers, proof-of-concept scenes, and social story systems.
2. **Balamonis Originals — owned IP and audience.** Release short films, pilots, anthology episodes, behind-the-scenes breakdowns, and repeatable story worlds.
3. **Balamonis Creator — software and scale.** Turn the internal production system into a browser-based workspace for briefs, story bibles, production, approvals, rights, and delivery.
4. **Balamonis+ — owned distribution.** Start only after the company has a catalog, returning viewers, direct paid demand, and complete rights clearance.

Do not compete by promising the largest list of AI models. Compete by turning a story or campaign brief into a consistent, rights-ready production—and then adapting that approved world into films, ads, trailers, and social episodes.

### Recommended positioning

> Balamonis turns a story or campaign brief into a consistent, rights-ready cinematic production—then adapts it for every screen without losing its soul.

### What Balamonis should become known for

- Long-form continuity across characters, locations, props, voices, color, and story logic.
- A visible record of human creative authorship and approvals.
- A rights ledger for every generated, licensed, performed, or sourced asset.
- Finished delivery, not just generation: edit, grade, sound, captions, textless masters, QC, and campaign variants.
- One approved story world converted into long-form, short-form, ads, stills, localized versions, and buyer packages.
- A recognizable creative voice: grounded emotion, cinematic restraint, and culturally specific storytelling.

## 6. Balamonis Creator: product plan

### The wedge

The first product is not a public prompt box. It is the best place to manage a multi-scene production from brief to approved delivery.

### User story

> A creator or team opens a project, defines the story and visual bible, plans scenes, estimates cost, creates or uploads takes, reviews continuity and rights, approves an edit, and exports the master plus platform variants.

### V1 workflow

```text
Project
→ brief
→ script
→ story + character bible
→ scene list
→ storyboard
→ shot cost estimate
→ generate or upload takes
→ continuity review
→ human approval
→ edit + finish
→ rights/QC review
→ master + social variants
```

### Product surfaces

| Surface | V1 responsibility |
|---|---|
| Project home | Status, owner, budget, next approval, recent outputs |
| Brief | Goal, audience, platform, duration, tone, references, rights needs |
| Story bible | Characters, locations, props, wardrobe, color, voice, continuity rules |
| Scene board | Ordered scenes, timing, intent, required assets, approved take |
| Generation queue | Provider, model, estimated cost, progress, retry, cancel |
| Review room | Side-by-side takes, comments, approval, rejection reason |
| Rights ledger | Source, license, consent, territory, term, model/provider, restrictions |
| Delivery center | Master, captions, audio stems, textless, vertical/square versions, QC |

### Build sequence

#### Phase 1: internal web control plane

- Authentication and organization/workspace.
- Project and role model.
- Brief, script, bible, scenes, and artifacts.
- Existing pipeline/checkpoint visualization.
- File upload to object storage.
- Cost estimation and manual provider selection.
- Human approval gates.

#### Phase 2: creator beta

- Generation queue and worker status.
- Review comments, approvals, and version restore.
- Continuity compare view.
- Exportable production packet and rights report.
- Billing guardrails and usage limits.
- Captions and format versions.

#### Phase 3: commercial platform

- Team workspaces and client review links.
- Reusable story/brand bibles.
- Provider routing by shot requirements, cost, latency, policy, and license.
- Campaign/version matrix.
- Audit logs, security controls, deletion/export workflows.
- Agency reporting and enterprise deployment.

### Technical architecture

```text
Vercel control plane
  ├─ Next.js web application
  ├─ authentication and project UI
  ├─ API for jobs, artifacts, approvals, and billing
  └─ signed uploads/downloads

PostgreSQL
  ├─ projects, scenes, decisions, approvals
  ├─ asset metadata and rights ledger
  └─ cost, status, audit events

Object storage
  ├─ source footage and references
  ├─ takes, proxies, masters, stems
  └─ manifests and delivery packages

Queued GPU workers
  ├─ isolated, pinned model environments
  ├─ generation and enhancement jobs
  ├─ progress, cancellation, retry
  └─ no arbitrary community nodes in production

Finishing workers
  ├─ FFmpeg, Remotion, HyperFrames
  ├─ caption/audio/color/QC
  └─ OpenTimelineIO interchange
```

Vercel should remain the control plane. GPU inference belongs on queued CUDA workers, not in serverless web functions.

## 7. Open-source cinematic stack: 2026 research

### Ranked production candidates

| Rank | Project | Recommended role | License/production note |
|---:|---|---|---|
| 1 | [Wan2.2](https://github.com/Wan-Video/Wan2.2) | Default local/cloud text-to-video and image-to-video; hero shots | Apache-2.0; 5B fits a 24GB GPU, larger models need high-memory workers |
| 2 | [LongCat-Video](https://github.com/meituan-longcat/LongCat-Video) | Longer shots, continuation, avatars, dialogue | MIT; promising for long-form continuity, but still a newer ecosystem |
| 3 | [SANA-Video / LongSANA](https://nvlabs.github.io/Sana/docs/sana_video/) | Fast 720p and 30–60 second experiments | Audit checkpoint and derived dependency licenses before commercial use |
| 4 | [LTX-2.5](https://github.com/Lightricks/LTX-2) | Synced sound, retakes, interpolation, HDR experiments | Community license can restrict competing services; negotiate before SaaS use |
| 5 | [SkyReels V3](https://github.com/SkyworkAI/SkyReels-V3) | Multi-reference identity and avatar experiments | Custom license needs counsel review |
| 6 | [HunyuanVideo-1.5](https://github.com/Tencent-Hunyuan/HunyuanVideo-1.5) | Efficient 720p and super-resolution R&D | Territory exclusions make it a poor global platform default |

### Recommended commercial pilot stack

- **Draft and standard motion:** Wan2.2-5B.
- **Selected final hero shots:** Wan2.2 A14B on rented 80GB workers.
- **Longer sequences and continuation:** LongCat-Video.
- **Dialogue/avatars:** LongCat Avatar or Wan2.2 S2V.
- **Voice:** [Chatterbox](https://github.com/resemble-ai/chatterbox) for consented voices.
- **Lip sync:** [MuseTalk](https://github.com/TMElyralab/MuseTalk) or [LatentSync](https://github.com/bytedance/LatentSync).
- **Music:** [ACE-Step](https://github.com/ace-step/ACE-Step-1.5), with track-level similarity and rights review.
- **Video upscaling:** [FlashVSR](https://github.com/OpenImagingLab/FlashVSR).
- **Frame interpolation:** [Practical-RIFE](https://github.com/hzwer/Practical-RIFE), used selectively and checked for ghosting.
- **Image enhancement:** [Real-ESRGAN](https://github.com/xinntao/Real-ESRGAN), not as the primary temporal video upscaler.
- **Timeline interchange:** [OpenTimelineIO](https://github.com/AcademySoftwareFoundation/OpenTimelineIO).
- **Finishing:** [FFmpeg](https://github.com/FFmpeg/FFmpeg), Remotion, HyperFrames, and professional finishing when required.
- **Provenance:** [C2PA Rust](https://github.com/contentauth/c2pa-rs).

### Do not use as a commercial default without additional clearance

- Original Wav2Lip weights: stated non-commercial/personal/research limits.
- MusicGen weights: non-commercial weights despite permissive surrounding code.
- LTX-2.5 in a competing creator SaaS without a commercial license.
- HunyuanVideo-1.5 for a global product without resolving its territory limits.
- Arbitrary ComfyUI community nodes in production.

### Honest Full HD and 4K pipeline

Most open models still create shots at 480p or 720p. The reliable production process is:

```text
short generated shot
→ artifact + continuity review
→ approved take
→ temporal video upscale
→ selective frame interpolation
→ 1080p or 2160p finishing master
→ grade, sound, captions, QC
```

Long-form quality comes from editorial construction and continuity control across many approved shots, not from asking one model for an entire movie.

### Compute reality

- **Daily local work:** one RTX 4090 24GB or RTX 5090 32GB for previews and smaller models.
- **Cloud burst:** 48GB workers for more demanding experiments.
- **Final hero work:** rented A100/H100 80GB workers.
- **Purchasing rule:** do not buy a cluster before consistent paid utilization exists.

Wan2.2 reports roughly nine minutes for one five-second 720p shot on an RTX 4090 for its 5B model. Twelve approved five-second shots make one finished minute; with 6–12 attempts per accepted shot, a finished minute can consume roughly 11–22 GPU-hours before sound, upscale, and revision. Track **cost per approved second**, not cost per generation.

## 8. Long-form production standard

### Required preproduction artifacts

- Logline and audience promise.
- Script and beat sheet.
- Story bible.
- Character, location, wardrobe, prop, and voice bible.
- Color and lens language.
- Scene geography and continuity map.
- Shot list with story purpose and expected length.
- Budget by scene and expected candidate count.
- Rights/consent plan.

### Shot record

Every approved shot should store:

- Project, scene, and shot IDs.
- Prompt, negative prompt, references, and reference hashes.
- Provider, model, version, workflow hash, seed, and date.
- Source resolution, delivery resolution, frame rate, and upscale method.
- Cost, generation duration, accepted/rejected state, and rejection reason.
- Character/location/wardrobe continuity tags.
- Rights, license, consent, territory, term, and permitted use.
- Human selections and creative notes.

### Master delivery standard

- Picture master in agreed aspect ratio and frame rate.
- ProRes/mezzanine master when required; H.264/H.265 viewing copies.
- Stereo mix and, when required, 5.1 plus M&E/stems.
- Captions/SDH, subtitles, textless elements, title/credit list.
- QC report for freeze frames, black frames, audio peaks, sync, and caption timing.
- Cue sheet, asset manifest, rights report, and AI-assistance disclosure.
- Trailer, poster, stills, synopsis, credits, and EPK for buyer-facing Originals.

## 10. Film sales, Netflix, and buyer readiness

Netflix does not accept unsolicited submissions. Its official route requires an agent, producer, attorney, manager, or industry executive with an existing Netflix relationship; completed films may also be discovered through established festivals and venues. See [Netflix submission guidance](https://help.netflix.com/en/node/100386).

### Realistic path

```text
excellent short or pilot
→ measurable audience response
→ festival/market visibility
→ producer, manager, attorney, or sales-agent relationship
→ buyer-ready package
→ requested pitch or finished-film acquisition conversation
```

### Buyer package

- Logline, one-page synopsis, treatment, and script.
- Pitch deck and sizzle/pilot.
- Budget top sheet, schedule, finance plan, comparable titles, and audience evidence.
- Talent attachments where appropriate.
- Chain-of-title report and human-authorship record.
- Rights matrix by territory, language, platform, term, and media.
- EPK, QC package, cue sheet, captions/subtitles, masters, and E&O insurance.

Do not make “sell to Netflix” the first-year KPI. Make **representable, rights-cleared, delivery-ready work with audience evidence** the first-year KPI.

## 11. Rights, safety, and legal operating checklist

Use qualified entertainment, IP, privacy, labor, and tax counsel. This plan is operational guidance, not legal advice.

- Form the operating company; consider a separate production entity for larger films.
- Secure options/assignments for underlying material and scripts.
- Use work-for-hire and IP assignment agreements for crew and contractors.
- Obtain performer, voice, likeness, digital-replica, location, artwork, trademark, and archive releases.
- Secure both composition/sync and recording/master rights for music.
- Archive the exact license and provider terms in effect when every asset is created.
- Keep model code, model weights, dependencies, output terms, territory rules, and revenue thresholds separate in the model registry.
- Preserve the human-written script, boards, camera plan, selects, compositing, edit, sound, and color decisions. The U.S. Copyright Office states that prompts alone generally do not establish sufficient human authorship for purely generated material. See its [AI Copyrightability Report](https://www.copyright.gov/ai/Copyright-and-Artificial-Intelligence-Part-2-Copyrightability-Report.pdf).
- Register scripts and finished films when appropriate.
- Determine SAG-AFTRA/signatory status before hiring covered performers.
- Carry E&O before professional distribution.
- Add product terms, privacy, consent/deletion controls, DMCA process, acceptable-use rules, security controls, and a clear policy for minors.
- Disclose sponsored work clearly in or near the content. See the [FTC influencer disclosure guide](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/disclosures-101-social-media-influencers).

### Non-negotiable human gates

- Script greenlight.
- Real-person voice/likeness consent.
- Provider/model or material production-path change.
- Rights and asset clearance.
- Final master approval.
- External publishing, spending, contracts, or buyer outreach.

## 17. OTT launch gate

Do not custom-build native OTT apps in year one. Validate paid demand using web rentals, memberships, screenings, or a hosted OTT service first.

Launch Balamonis+ only when at least four of these are true:

- 10,000+ owned email/community members.
- 3,000+ monthly returning long-form viewers.
- 500+ people have paid for screenings, rentals, memberships, or preorders.
- 25–50 hours of owned or securely licensed programming.
- At least 12 credible releases scheduled per year.
- $5,000+ monthly direct paid-content demand.
- Beta subscriber monthly churn below 7%.
- Rights cleared for intended territories and platforms.

Start with the web. Add TV and mobile applications only after paid retention is proven.

### Kill rules

- Stop a service offer after five qualified pitches with no purchase unless the message or scope clearly failed the test.
- Stop an Original after the proof if the story fails a human audience review; do not spend more to rescue weak fundamentals.
- Do not build an OTT feature without a direct-demand metric tied to it.
